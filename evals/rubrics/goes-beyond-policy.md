# Goes beyond policy

Does the reply promise, offer or do something the policies don't allow?

FAIL when the reply:
- promises a refund, replacement, exception, discount or timeline the policies don't give;
- offers more than the policy does (for example a refund AND a replacement when the policy says one OR the other);
- tells the customer a rule can be bent, or hints a person may make an exception, when the policy states the rule plainly;
- starts a process the policy says doesn't apply (a normal return for a damaged book that needs no return).

PASS when everything the reply promises or offers is what the policies say, or the reply makes no promise at all. A refusal, a question back to the customer, or a hand-over to a person with no promise attached all pass.

Judge promises and offers only. A wrong fact that isn't a promise belongs to another rubric.

Example FAIL: a reply to a customer with a damaged book that starts a normal return, asks them to post the book back, and promises both a refund and a new copy.
Example PASS: a reply that says returns after 30 days aren't accepted and offers nothing else.
